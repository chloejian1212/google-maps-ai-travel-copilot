# Audience, assumptions, and scope

## Intended audience

The Sprint 4 reflection identifies young people who enjoy travel, planning their own itineraries, and documenting experiences for friends or social media. It describes the designer's observation that planning takes time and involves switching between apps.

These observations frame the design opportunity. They are not results from an interview study or a measured survey in the supplied materials.

## Design hypothesis

Keeping destination selection, route creation, trip review, and sharing in a familiar map context could make the planning journey feel more coherent. This is the concept to evaluate, not a validated improvement.

The reflection mentions outside research about Gen Z travelers' preference for convenience and digital support, but does not identify a publication or provide a citation. This repository therefore does not repeat a demographic statistic or treat that statement as independently verified evidence.

## Evidence supplied

- A five-page Sprint 4 submission with hand-drawn ideation sketches and the designer's reflections.
- A Figma design file containing pages named Ideas & Inspiration, Wireframes, and Mockups.
- Eleven final mobile mockups and eleven full mobile wireframes, exported from Figma and visually reviewed.
- A Figma prototype whose primary route flow and sharing-sheet transition were clicked directly.

## Boundaries

The repository documents a UI/UX concept. It does not contain a working route-generation model, Google Maps integration, backend, API credentials, or a deployable application. A chat conversation or a generated route visible in a mockup is illustrative interface content.

No participant dataset, task-completion rate, usability benchmark, accessibility audit, or final-design comparison study is documented in the supplied submission. The project does not claim measured time savings, verified recommendation quality, or improved travel outcomes.

## Questions for a future evaluation

- Can users distinguish selecting places from asking the copilot to generate a route?
- Can they understand and edit the inputs before accepting a proposed itinerary?
- Are uncertain or unavailable opening hours, transport options, and recommendations clear?
- Can users tell which trip details will be shared, and cancel or change the sharing destination?
- Can the interface work with larger text, assistive technology, and interrupted connectivity?

These are proposed next steps, not completed research activities.
